## Where this batch's evidence is genuinely hard — plan 10's C5 rows

This is the part worth your variation budget, and the part most likely to produce a wrong verdict
cheaply. Read plan 10 §6's preamble in full before you touch C5.

The C5 rows assert **three serialization orders** that MC-11 / §14F F6 distinguish. The projection
found and the owner ruled that the original fixtures were unbuildable, for two reasons worth
knowing because they will occur to you too:

1. **There is no seam that lets one session hold a row lock open for another.** Both participants
   run whole production commands that take and release their own locks inside `maybe_begin`.
2. **There is no separate post-lock re-read statement to skip.** The re-read *is*
   `_locks.py:22-41`'s `populate_existing=True`, which refreshes the identity-mapped instance in
   place. Deleting it changes every locking caller in the project — that mutant is not local to
   this row.

**The replacement choreography:** a third **referee** session takes the row's `FOR UPDATE` and
holds it; each participant's command is started as a task and **observed to block**; the referee
commits and both are awaited. Postgres queues the waiters in arrival order, so the order is
**forced, not raced**. The precedent is shipped and APPROVED: `test_apply_stock_demand.py:821-867`.
**A barrier is not used** — it releases both sides at once and expresses a race, not an order.

**Both observed blocks are assertions of the row, not setup.** They are what proves the order held
*and* that each session finished its unlocked discovery read before the other committed. A C5 test
that does not observe both blocks has not forced its order, whatever it asserts afterwards — and
your job on C5 is exactly to show that each test **forces** its order, i.e. that the cell's
mutation produces the wrong outcome **deterministically**. The B1 lesson is the one to avoid: a
lock test that ran entirely inside one transaction and therefore could not fail.

If the choreography cannot be built as written, that is a **BLOCKED** row and a card — not a
variant you improvise.
